What this audit is based on

My session couldn't reach the domain directly, so this is built from screenshots of three pages. That's enough to diagnose the big structural problems, but it leaves real gaps — I've listed both sides honestly so you know which recommendations are grounded and which need a look before you act.

Reviewed

  • Homepage — full-bleed KhemisTree logo, one button labeled "VLTRN"
  • VLTRN page — album cover, "PRE ORDER" button, nav (Home / VLTRN / Shop)
  • Shop — 3×3 grid, 9 SKUs, "Buy Now" on every card
  • Bundle artwork — contents, pricing and shipping terms read off the graphics

Not seen — verify before acting

  • Product detail pages (or whether they exist at all)
  • Cart and checkout flow
  • Mobile rendering — likely where most of your traffic lands
  • Footer, policies, page speed, analytics setup

The core problem

You are asking people to spend up to $299 on a record they cannot hear, from a site that never tells them who made it, what they get, or when it arrives. Every piece of persuasion on this store — the bundle contents, the savings, the shipping window, the free digital download — is baked into JPEGs that go unreadable at thumbnail size and vanish on a phone.

The artwork is genuinely strong; that's not the issue. The issue is that artwork is doing a job it cannot do alone. A fan arriving from TikTok has maybe eight seconds of patience, and right now those eight seconds contain a logo and a button labeled with a word they may not recognize. Nearly everything below is a variation on one fix: put the selling into text, above the buttons, in the order a stranger needs it.

Findings

Ranked by expected impact on orders, not by how easy they are.

01

The store has almost no words on it

Critical1–2 days
Observed Homepage: 1 image + 1 button ("VLTRN"). VLTRN page: 1 image + 1 button ("PRE ORDER"). Shop: 9 titles + 9 prices. Total body copy across all three pages: effectively zero.

A visitor cannot learn who Cambatta, Red Pillar and Blue Pillar are, what VLTRN is, when it ships, what the free digital download includes, or why a $33 CD costs $33. There is nothing to build desire with, and nothing to overcome hesitation with.

This also silently costs you traffic: Google has no text to index, so searches for the artists' names don't lead here. Link previews shared to Discord, iMessage and Twitter come back empty. Screen readers get nothing at all.

Fix

Add real copy in three places, in this priority order:

  • Homepage hero — headline, one-line description, release date, two buttons
  • VLTRN page — the story of the record, tracklist, credits, format comparison
  • Each product card — one line of what's included, under the title
02

Nobody can hear the album

CriticalHours

There is no audio anywhere on the site. You're selling music the way you'd sell a t-shirt with no photo. Pre-orders convert on one feeling — I've heard this and I need to own it — and you've removed the only thing that produces it.

Fix

Embed a player directly above the buy buttons on both the VLTRN page and the shop. A released single is ideal; a 90-second medley across three tracks works nearly as well. Add streaming links (Spotify / Apple / YouTube) beneath it — you lose a few clicks to streaming, but you convert far more of the people who were never going to buy blind.

03

Every selling point is trapped inside an image

Critical1 day
Trapped in the bundle JPEGs Contents · regular price $143 / $341 · bundle price $129 / $299 · "SAVE $14 (10%)" · "SAVE $52 (15%)" · "FREE DIGITAL DOWNLOAD" · "ALLOW 10–14 BUSINESS DAYS" · "YOU CHOOSE! SELECT THE HAT AND T-SHIRT YOU WANT" · "MOST COMPLETE BUNDLE"

That is your entire sales argument, and none of it is text. At grid thumbnail size the type in those graphics is already hard to read on a desktop monitor; on a phone it's illegible. So the shop grid effectively communicates nine names and nine prices — with the $299 bundle looking like a wildly expensive mystery instead of a $341 package at 15% off.

Fix

Keep the graphics as the product image, but restate every one of those facts as HTML text on the card and the product page. Text is readable at any size, indexable, translatable, and screen-reader accessible. The image should reinforce the words, never replace them.

04

A pre-order with no date and no scarcity

HighHours

The button says "PRE ORDER" but nothing says pre-order until when, or ships on what date. A pre-order without a deadline is just a purchase a fan can safely postpone — and postponed purchases mostly don't happen.

Fix
  • State the release date everywhere the pre-order button appears
  • Add a countdown to the pre-order cutoff
  • Cap the vinyl at a stated number and show it ("300 pressed · 112 left") — vinyl scarcity is real and fans respond to it
  • Give pre-orderers something the post-release buyer won't get: instant digital, a signed insert, name in the liner notes
05

The homepage spends your best screen on a logo

HighHours

The full viewport is one logo image. The only call to action sits below it, labeled "VLTRN" — a noun, not an action. A first-time visitor has no idea whether that's a product, a person, or a section of the site. Meanwhile the shop — the only page that takes money — is reachable only through the nav.

Fix

Shrink the logo to a header element and rebuild the hero around the album: headline, one-line subhead, release date, and two buttons — "Pre-Order the Album" and "Hear the Single". The homepage's job is to route people to the shop in one click, not to display branding.

06

"Buy Now" on every card suggests there's nowhere to sell

High1–2 days

All nine cards use the same "Buy Now" label. If that jumps straight to checkout, two things break: apparel buyers have no visible place to choose size and color (your own bundle art promises "you choose"), and you've skipped the one page where a $299 purchase actually gets justified. If it does open a product page, the label is simply lying about what happens next.

Fix

Give every SKU a real product page carrying:

  • Multiple photos — front, back, detail, and worn for apparel
  • A size chart with actual measurements (this is the top return-driver on merch)
  • What's included, written out, plus the digital download terms
  • Ship window and pre-order date restated next to the button

Reserve "Buy Now" for the product page itself; the grid says "View Details".

07

No social proof of any kind

HighOngoing

No reviews, no fan quotes, no stream counts, no press, no pre-order tally, no photos of anyone wearing the merch. For an independent release on an unfamiliar domain, the buyer's live question is "is this real and will it actually ship?" — and the site currently offers no evidence either way.

Fix
  • Pull three strong comments from TikTok/IG on the announcement and run them as quotes
  • Show a live pre-order count once it's respectable ("412 fans have pre-ordered")
  • Post fulfillment photos — packed boxes, the vinyl test pressing — as proof of a real operation
  • Turn on review capture now so the post-release wave has somewhere to land
08

No trust signals and no visible policies

HighHalf a day

I saw no footer content, no returns or exchange policy, no shipping policy in text, no contact route, no secure-checkout indication. A $299 order from a brand-new storefront needs all of these, and their absence is felt most sharply by exactly the high-value buyers you most want.

Fix

Build a real footer: contact email, returns and exchanges, shipping and delivery, privacy, and social links. Add a short FAQ answering the four questions fans are actually asking — when does it ship, what is the digital download and when do I get it, how do sizes run, what if it arrives damaged.

09

Nothing captures the visitors who don't buy today

HighHours

There's no email or SMS capture anywhere. The large majority of first-time visitors won't buy on that visit — and right now every one of them leaves permanently. For a release campaign, the list is the asset: it's what you own on drop day, on tour announcement day, and on the next project.

Fix
  • Offer a track download or an unreleased snippet in exchange for an email
  • Add SMS for the drop alert — for music, SMS routinely outperforms email on launch day
  • Turn on abandoned-cart email if the platform supports it; it's the single highest-return automation in ecommerce
  • Install the Meta and TikTok pixels now, before the traffic push, so you can retarget
10

The bundle ladder is invisible where it matters

MediumHours

Your three-tier structure is well built — $69 entry, $129 middle, $299 flagship is a textbook ladder where the top tier makes the middle look reasonable. But the grid shows only the final prices. The anchors ($143, $341), the savings, and the "most complete" flag all live inside the artwork, so the mechanism that makes the ladder work never reaches the shopper.

Fix
  • Show the struck-through regular price next to every bundle price as text
  • Badge the Collector bundle "Most Popular" — it's your margin sweet spot and the tier the ladder is designed to sell
  • Add a small comparison table: three columns, rows for CD / cassette / vinyl / tee / hat / jersey / digital, ticks in the cells
11

Your strongest hook is buried in the artwork

MediumUnder an hour

"Free digital download with every purchase" resolves the biggest objection to buying physical music in 2026 — I want the vinyl but I need to actually listen to it today. It appears only as small type inside three bundle graphics, and nowhere on the six individual SKUs.

Fix

Put it as a text badge on all nine cards, in the hero, and next to every buy button. Say when it arrives — "download link the moment you order" is far stronger than "free digital download," if that's how it works.

12

The bundle discounts are too thin to motivate

MediumHalf a day

10% off the Starter and Collector isn't a reason to trade up — a fan who wanted the CD saves $8 and mostly notices they're spending $69 instead of $33. Discount is the weakest bundle lever available to an artist, because it competes on the one axis where you can't win.

Fix

Make bundles worth buying for what they contain, not what they save: a colorway sold only in the bundle, a signed insert, a numbered certificate, early access to the digital, entry to a listening call. Exclusivity moves collectors; 10% doesn't.

13

Mobile is unverified and structurally at risk

MediumInvestigate

I couldn't see the mobile rendering, but two things in this build reliably break down on a phone: a full-viewport hero image that pushes the only button off-screen, and dense infographic thumbnails whose type shrinks to noise. Given that your traffic almost certainly arrives from Instagram and TikTok in-app browsers, mobile is the store.

Fix

Test the whole flow on a real phone through the Instagram in-app browser, not just a desktop resize. Confirm a buy button is visible without scrolling on the hero, check the shop grid drops to one or two columns, and time the checkout end to end.

One thing not to change

Your prices — $33, $44, $55, $77, $99 — are numerologically consistent, and that is obviously deliberate given the Kemetic and alchemical language running through the brand. If anyone tells you to "optimize" these to $34.99 and $49.99, ignore them. Charm pricing would flatten a distinctive brand signal for a rounding error, and your buyers are collectors, not bargain hunters.

The real work isn't lowering these numbers — it's earning them with copy. A $33 CD is roughly double the market rate, and that's completely defensible once the page says what's in the package. It is indefensible when the page says only "$33.00".

What to measure

Without these you're guessing. Four numbers tell you which stage is actually leaking.

Add-to-cart rate
Low means the problem is upstream — the pages aren't selling. That's findings 01–06.
Cart abandonment
High means people want it but something at checkout stops them: shipping cost surprise, trust, or friction.
Mobile vs. desktop conversion
If mobile lags badly, finding 13 jumps to the top of this list and stays there.
Bundle mix
Share of orders by tier tells you whether the ladder is working. If Starter dominates, the middle tier isn't being merchandised.

Based on screenshots of three pages, reviewed August 2026. Findings 01–12 are grounded in what was directly observable; finding 13 is a structural risk I flagged but could not confirm. Product pages, cart, checkout and mobile rendering weren't available for review — if you can share those, the checkout half of the funnel is worth auditing on its own.
